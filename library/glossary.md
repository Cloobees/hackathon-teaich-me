## model
The Claude version that does the work. Haiku is fast and small, Sonnet is the everyday choice, Opus is the most capable.

## effort
How much thinking Claude does before it answers. Higher effort is slower and costs more, and helps on harder problems.

## context window
The amount of text Claude can hold in mind in one conversation: your messages, its replies, files and tool results. When it fills up, older material drops out.

## prompt
The instruction you give Claude. A clear prompt says the goal, the context and the format you want back.

## Project
A workspace in Claude with its own instructions and files that every chat inside it can use.

## skill
A folder with a `SKILL.md` file that teaches Claude how to do one job. Claude loads it when the job comes up.

## connector
A link between Claude and another tool, such as Slack, Context Builder, GitHub or Atlassian, so Claude can read or act there with your permission.

## MCP
Model Context Protocol. The open standard most connectors use to give Claude tools and data.

## memory
What Claude keeps between chats about you and your work, so you do not repeat yourself.

## agent
Claude working through several steps on its own, using tools, until a task is done.

## token
A small piece of text, roughly a short word or part of one. Models read and write in tokens, and limits and costs are counted in them.

## Personal Workspace
Your own Context Builder workspace. Only you can see it. Its name starts with `my-workspace@` followed by your email.

## scope
Where Context Builder knowledge lives: your personal workspace, a team workspace, a project, or global.

## node
One typed item in the Context Builder knowledge graph, such as a note or a task, with metadata and content.
